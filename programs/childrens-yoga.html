FAQs

Questions parents often ask

Which age group is the programme for?

The programme is designed for children aged 7 to 13 years.

How is this different from a regular yoga or exercise class?

Classical Hatha Yoga is a structured system that goes well beyond physical exercise. Children learn specific postures and practices with attention to alignment, stability and awareness. The programme follows the structure laid out by Sadhguru himself.

Is it safe for my child, especially in an online class?

Not every yoga posture is meant for everybody. These days many classes teach children all kinds of postures, but a child’s body is still developing and should not attempt all of them. This programme makes that distinction clearly and teaches only practices suited to this age. The class is live and the batch is small, so the teacher can watch and guide each child as they practise.

What will my child learn?

Children are introduced to suitable asanas, pranayama, chanting and meditation. The classes also include fun games and interaction with other children of the same age and similar interest. Simple good habits, such as how time in nature and playing in the mud benefit them, are woven into the games.

Is it suitable for beginners? Does my child need to be flexible?

Yes, it is suitable for beginners and no previous yoga experience is needed. Children do not need to be flexible to start. In fact, the programme helps them become more flexible over time.

Can my child join if they have a medical or physical condition?

Please write to the teacher about any such concern, and she will guide you on how your child can take part.

Does a parent need to stay with the child during the class?

No. Once the class setup is ready, children can be on their own in the room. It usually works better that way.

What if my child misses a class?

Inform the teacher in advance. If the reason is genuine, she can arrange a backup class so your child can continue with the rest of the batch.

Does my child need to practise between classes?

Yes. Children practise together on Sundays and are encouraged to practise on their own, if not daily then at least three days a week. The teacher keeps track of how each child is doing.
